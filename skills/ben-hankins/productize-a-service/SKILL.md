**When to use this skill:** similar custom work sells repeatedly but delivery still scales mainly
through owner hours or added headcount.

**What it produces:** a standardized offer, explicit exception rules, manual MVP, systemized
delivery, automation priorities, recurring model, and retention feedback loop.

## Find the repeatable core

Review profitable engagements for repeated buyer, problem, inputs, decisions, deliverables, and
outcome. Separate valuable judgment from accidental customization. Productization begins where
customers repeatedly pay for the same transformation, not where work is easiest to automate.

## Define the standard and the boundary

Specify the promised outcome, inputs, stages, service level, price, and explicit exclusions. Create
a small number of supported variants only where they change value materially. Custom work earns an
exception when it validates a product direction, commands a real premium, or creates strategic
learning; otherwise it creates hidden operating debt.

## Prove the manual product

Deliver the standardized offer manually to real customers before building software. Measure time,
quality, failure, support, and willingness to renew. Remove steps customers do not value and make
the repeatable decisions legible enough to transfer.

## Systemize before automating

Document inputs, handoffs, quality checks, owners, and exception paths. Automate stable, frequent,
low-judgment work after the process survives repetition. Preserve high-value relationships,
creative judgment, and core value delivery until their quality model is understood.

## Design for retention and independence

Choose recurring components only when ongoing value genuinely recurs. Track activation, outcome,
renewal, support burden, margin, and owner intervention. Fix churn and exception volume before
scaling acquisition. The test is whether trained operators can deliver consistently without the
founder acting as invisible middleware.

## What good looks like

Customers receive a consistent outcome, delivery margin improves with volume, exceptions are rare
and priced, and recurring revenue corresponds to recurring value. The owner can step away without
quality collapsing, and automation removes known repetition rather than encoding a broken process.

## Hard rules

- NEVER automate a process before its value and quality are understood.
- NEVER call recurring billing recurring value.
- NEVER accept custom work that silently changes the standard offer.
- ALWAYS test the standardized offer with paying customers.
- ALWAYS measure retention, margin, and owner intervention before scaling.
