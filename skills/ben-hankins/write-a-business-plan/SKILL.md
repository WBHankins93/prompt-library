**When to use this skill:** a venture thesis must become a coherent operating and funding plan that
others can evaluate and the team can execute.

**What it produces:** an evidence-backed plan covering customer, offer, distribution, operations,
economics, milestones, funding, risks, ownership, and validation gates.

## Write for the decision

Identify the reader and decision: internal commitment, loan, investment, partnership, or execution
alignment. Lead with the venture, customer problem, proposed mechanism, evidence, and requested
decision. Omit generic industry prose that does not affect the case.

## Connect market to action

Define the first customer, current alternative, urgency, buying process, competition, positioning,
and route to market. Distinguish total market from the segment reachable with current channels and
capacity. Cite evidence and label assumptions.

## Make the operating model concrete

Describe how demand becomes delivery: capabilities, people, partners, systems, dependencies,
quality control, and capacity constraints. Assign milestone owners and dates. Show what changes as
volume grows and where additional capital or hiring becomes necessary.

## Build financials from drivers

Tie revenue to units, price, conversion, and retention; tie cost to delivery and operating choices.
Include base and downside cash, break-even, working capital, funding amount, and use of funds.
Explain the few assumptions that dominate the model.

## Expose risk and learning

List commercial, execution, financial, legal, and dependency risks with mitigation and early
signals. Sequence milestones so the plan buys down its largest uncertainty before expensive
commitment. State what evidence requires revision or stopping.

## What good looks like

Every major number connects to an operating driver, every milestone has an owner, and the funding
request connects to a measurable reduction in risk or increase in capacity. A skeptical reader can
find assumptions quickly, while the team can use the same document to decide what happens next.

## Hard rules

- NEVER use unsupported market size as proof of demand.
- NEVER present financial outputs without their operating drivers.
- NEVER hide material risk to make the narrative persuasive.
- ALWAYS connect funding to specific milestones and learning.
- ALWAYS state assumptions separately from evidence.
