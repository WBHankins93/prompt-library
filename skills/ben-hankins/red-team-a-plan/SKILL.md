**When to use this skill:** a team is converging on a consequential plan and agreement may be
hiding untested assumptions, incentives, or irreversible downside.

**What it produces:** a steel-manned opposing case, assumption and incentive map, pre-mortem,
disconfirming evidence, mitigations, and stop conditions.

## Reconstruct the plan fairly

State the objective, chosen mechanism, assumptions, constraints, owners, and evidence in terms its
advocates recognize. Separate disagreement about goals from disagreement about means. A red team
that attacks a weaker version produces theater, not decision quality.

## Attack assumptions and incentives

List what must be true across demand, capability, timing, behavior, law, economics, and operations.
Ask who benefits from the plan, who bears downside, what information each party controls, and what
success metric could be gamed. Rank assumptions by impact, uncertainty, and detectability.

## Run the failure pre-mortem

Assume the plan failed materially and work backward through plausible causal chains. Include
coordination failure, adoption, cash, dependencies, reputation, legal exposure, and second-order
effects. Identify early signals that would appear before the final outcome.

## Seek disconfirming evidence

Name evidence that would change the recommendation and look for it before collecting more support.
Compare with base rates and analogous attempts while preserving relevant differences. Distinguish
an absent signal from evidence of absence.

## Improve or stop the plan

For each highest-ranked risk, choose avoid, reduce, transfer, monitor, or accept. Add reversible
stages and decision gates where learning can precede commitment. Define stop conditions with owners
and dates. If mitigations remove the plan's economics or purpose, recommend against it plainly.

## What good looks like

Advocates agree their plan was represented accurately, the strongest failure paths are visible,
and the decision changes through better evidence, smaller commitment, stronger mitigation, or a
clear stop. The exercise reduces confidence when warranted and increases it only when the plan
survives specific challenges.

## Hard rules

- NEVER attack a plan weaker than the version its advocates would defend.
- NEVER confuse skepticism with insight; every challenge needs a causal path.
- NEVER bury legal, incentive, or reputation risk beneath technical feasibility.
- ALWAYS seek evidence that could reverse the recommendation.
- ALWAYS define stop conditions before commitment escalates.
