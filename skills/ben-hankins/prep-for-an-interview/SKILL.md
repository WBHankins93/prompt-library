**When to use this skill:** a candidate knows the role and interview format and needs to make
relevant judgment, evidence, and communication reliable under pressure.

**What it produces:** a scorecard, evidence story bank, drills, gap responses, interviewer
questions, and rehearsal plan targeted to the actual evaluation.

## Reconstruct the evaluation

Map each interview stage to what it likely tests: capability, judgment, collaboration, motivation,
communication, or risk. Use the role, interviewer function, and format. Prepare for the decision
criteria, not a generic list of popular questions.

## Build a flexible evidence bank

Select examples covering impact, conflict, ambiguity, failure, influence, learning, and a
role-specific hard problem. For each, know the context, personal contribution, decision, tradeoff,
result, and reflection. Prepare evidence atoms that can answer several questions without forcing
the same polished story everywhere.

## Drill the reasoning surface

Practice the tasks closest to the role: system or case decomposition, portfolio defense, research
discussion, operating scenario, or technical pattern. Explain assumptions and tradeoffs aloud.
When stuck, demonstrate how you clarify and recover; interviewers evaluate the path, not only the
final answer.

## Prepare risks without defensiveness

Name likely concerns—gap, transition, short tenure, failure, compensation, or level—and answer with
truth, context, evidence of learning, and relevance. Do not over-explain. Rehearse concise openings
and closings, then vary question wording to prevent memorized delivery.

## Interview the employer

Prepare questions that reveal success measures, decision authority, team constraints, operating
reality, and why the role is open. Use answers to assess fit, not merely signal enthusiasm.

## What good looks like

The candidate can adapt evidence to unfamiliar wording, explain decisions rather than recite
scripts, and recover visibly from uncertainty. Answers are specific, personal contribution is
clear, risks are addressed without evasion, and the candidate leaves with better evidence about
whether the role fits.

## Hard rules

- NEVER script answers word for word.
- NEVER claim team outcomes as individual ownership.
- NEVER hide a known gap behind jargon.
- ALWAYS practice reasoning aloud under realistic constraints.
- ALWAYS prepare questions that test the employer's fit.
