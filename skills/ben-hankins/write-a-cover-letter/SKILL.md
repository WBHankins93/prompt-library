**When to use this skill:** the application needs a short argument connecting the candidate's real
evidence and motivation to this employer's particular need.

**What it produces:** a tailored hiring thesis, two supported claims, credible motivation, any
necessary transition context, and a concise close.

## Decide whether the letter has a job

Use a letter to explain a transition, unusual fit, mission connection, referral, portfolio thread,
or evidence the resume fragments. If it can only restate job history and enthusiasm, omit it unless
required.

## Build one argument

Identify the employer's likely problem and the candidate's strongest relevant pattern. Open with
that connection rather than "I am writing to apply." Make one thesis the letter can prove in a few
paragraphs.

## Support claims with selective evidence

Choose two examples that show outcome, judgment, and personal contribution. Add only context needed
to understand the relevance. Use numbers when verified, but let consequence carry the sentence
when precision is unavailable.

## Make motivation specific

Explain why this work, team, product, or moment matters using observable detail. Avoid praise that
could be sent unchanged to a competitor. When addressing a gap or transition, be brief, factual,
and forward-looking; do not turn the letter into a defense.

## Close with confidence and restraint

Reinforce the contribution and invite the next conversation. Keep the letter comfortably within one
page, remove duplicated resume content, and read it aloud for human cadence.

## What good looks like

The letter adds a reason to interview that the resume alone does not provide. Every employer detail
is relevant, every claim has evidence, and the candidate sounds like a specific person rather than
a template. Removing the company name would break the argument.

## Hard rules

- NEVER fabricate personal connection, motivation, or employer knowledge.
- NEVER repeat the resume paragraph by paragraph.
- NEVER use generic praise as evidence of fit.
- ALWAYS support the hiring thesis with verified examples.
- ALWAYS cut the letter if it has no distinct job.
