**When to use this skill:** a financial outcome looks precise but the decision depends on whether
its underlying operating assumptions survive reality.

**What it produces:** a driver model, evidence grades, scenario and sensitivity analysis, cash-risk
view, and proof requirements for the assumptions that matter most.

## Rebuild from operating drivers

Translate totals into units: customers, price, volume, conversion, retention, gross margin, hiring,
working capital, and timing. Separate facts, contracted amounts, observed rates, estimates, and
aspirations. A spreadsheet formula does not turn an unsupported input into evidence.

## Test internal consistency

Check whether acquisition supports projected customers, staffing supports delivery, infrastructure
supports volume, and timing respects sales cycles and cash collection. Compare implied rates with
history and relevant reference ranges, then explain material departures rather than normalizing
them away.

## Build asymmetric scenarios

Create base, upside, and downside cases by changing coherent drivers, not applying one percentage
to every line. Downside should include slower revenue, compressed margin, delayed collection, and
costs that cannot fall immediately. Identify the cash trough, runway, and point where a reversible
plan becomes forced.

## Find the load-bearing assumptions

Vary one driver at a time and rank its effect on cash, return, and break-even. Then test correlated
failures. Focus diligence on assumptions that combine high impact with weak evidence. A small error
in a dominant driver matters more than perfect precision elsewhere.

## Convert uncertainty into proof

For each load-bearing assumption, name the evidence, owner, deadline, and decision it unlocks.
Recommend staged capital or milestones when uncertainty can be bought down. State the conditions
under which the opportunity remains attractive and the conditions that require walking away.

## What good looks like

The forecast tells a coherent operating story, reveals its cash trough, and shows which assumptions
control the decision. Downside is survivable or explicitly unacceptable. Decision-makers can see
where evidence ends, what must be proven next, and how much capital is at risk before learning.

## Hard rules

- NEVER confuse formula precision with forecast confidence.
- NEVER present upside without a coherent downside and cash trough.
- NEVER average away a load-bearing risk.
- ALWAYS grade the evidence behind material assumptions.
- ALWAYS state the condition that invalidates the investment case.
