**When to use this skill:** a real upcoming demand requires faster improvement than passive study
or unstructured repetition will produce.

**What it produces:** a performance target, skill and bottleneck map, practice-feedback loop,
retrieval schedule, transfer projects, and evidence-based adjustment criteria.

## Define observable performance

Specify what the learner must do, under what conditions, to what standard, and by when. Use a
representative task and baseline attempt. "Understand" is not a target until it changes performance.

## Decompose and find the bottleneck

Break the skill into knowledge, perception, decisions, execution, and communication. Diagnose the
current limiting component from errors and latency. Practice the bottleneck while maintaining the
minimum surrounding context needed for transfer.

## Build short deliberate loops

Use attempts near the edge of current ability with immediate evidence, correction, and another
attempt. Isolate recurring error patterns, then reintegrate them into the whole task. Prefer frequent
focused practice to occasional marathons.

## Retrieve and transfer

Recall models without notes, explain them, vary examples, and schedule revisits after forgetting
begins. Apply the skill in increasingly realistic projects with time, ambiguity, and quality
constraints. Consuming more material is not progress when performance stays unchanged.

## Use failure as data

After attempts, separate knowledge gaps, flawed models, execution slips, and environmental causes.
Change one training variable at a time. Seek feedback from the work product or a credible reviewer,
not reassurance. Record evidence against the baseline.

## What good looks like

Performance improves on representative unseen tasks, errors become more specific, and the learner
can explain and adapt the governing model. Practice time targets the current bottleneck, feedback
arrives quickly, and the plan changes when evidence shows the constraint moved.

## Hard rules

- NEVER use hours studied as the primary measure of skill.
- NEVER repeat full tasks when one component causes most failure.
- NEVER confuse recognition with recall or transfer.
- ALWAYS baseline performance before designing practice.
- ALWAYS change the training plan when evidence shows a new bottleneck.
