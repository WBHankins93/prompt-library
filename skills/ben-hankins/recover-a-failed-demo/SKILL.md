**When to use this skill:** a customer-facing technical moment has failed and the quality of your
response now matters more than pretending the failure did not happen.

**What it produces:** an immediate response, a bounded explanation, a recovery path tied to the
customer's decision, and a documented follow-through plan.

## Stabilize the room before the system

Stop the failing path once repetition stops producing information. State what everyone can observe,
separate it from what you do not yet know, and protect the remaining meeting time. Frantic retries
signal that the plan depended on luck. Calmly choose whether to diagnose briefly, switch to a known
fallback, or continue the decision conversation without the live path.

## Own the failure without inventing a cause

Use direct language: what failed, what impact it has on the promised outcome, and what you will
investigate. Ownership is not a dramatic apology or self-blame. It is the absence of deflection.
Never blame the customer's environment, network, or data until evidence supports that conclusion.
If a product limit surfaced, name it as a boundary rather than disguising it as a transient bug.

## Preserve the decision, not the script

Ask what the audience still needs to learn to make the next decision. A screenshot, recorded run,
architecture walkthrough, or smaller live proof may establish the relevant behavior without
pretending it replaces what failed. Skip material that no longer serves that decision. A recovery
that finishes every slide but leaves the central question unanswered has not recovered.

## Set a credible recovery contract

Before the meeting ends, agree on:

- the unanswered question and who owns it;
- the evidence you will provide;
- a realistic delivery time;
- whether another live session is necessary;
- what happens if the limitation is confirmed.

Choose a deadline you can beat. Send the written recap while the shared understanding is fresh.

## Rebuild trust through execution

Reproduce the failure, isolate its layer, and report the cause in proportion to the stakes. Include
the fix or boundary, proof that it was verified, and any scope consequence. Close the loop with every
stakeholder who saw the failure. Trust returns when the recovery is specific, timely, and complete,
not when the explanation sounds sophisticated.

## What good looks like

The customer can distinguish the observed failure from speculation, knows what remains unproven,
and sees a recovery path that respects their decision. You neither minimized the problem nor let it
consume the engagement. The follow-up arrives when promised with evidence strong enough to restore
technical confidence—even if the honest answer is that the product cannot meet the requirement.

## Hard rules

- NEVER keep retrying merely to avoid acknowledging failure.
- NEVER invent a root cause in front of the customer.
- NEVER hide a product boundary behind vague language about configuration.
- ALWAYS tie the recovery to the unanswered customer decision.
- ALWAYS close the loop with evidence, not reassurance alone.
