**When to use this skill:** a client intends to begin a project and ambiguity about scope,
responsibilities, access, or feedback would become delivery risk.

**What it produces:** a go/no-go fit decision, explicit scope and dependencies, commercial
readiness, secure access, working agreements, and a confirmed first milestone.

## Confirm fit before creating momentum

Recheck the desired outcome, decision authority, budget, deadline driver, internal capacity, and
prior attempts. Surface warning signs: no accountable owner, incompatible expectations, withheld
access, a deadline with no cause, or disrespect for boundaries. Decline, re-scope, or price the risk
before kickoff; onboarding does not transform a poor fit into a healthy engagement.

## Make the work boundary legible

Document the outcome, included deliverables, explicit exclusions, assumptions, acceptance criteria,
milestones, revision policy, and change process. List client-provided content, decisions, access,
and their due dates. A dependency without an owner and date is an invisible schedule risk.

## Establish commercial readiness

Do not begin delivery until the agreement is signed and the required initial payment is received.
Confirm payment schedule, ownership transfer, termination, change orders, delay consequences, and
the relationship between scope and price. Keep legal terms consistent with the proposal rather than
negotiating a new project through the contract.

## Turn kickoff into an operating agreement

Use kickoff to confirm roles, the single accountable approver, communication channel, update
cadence, feedback format, decision turnaround, escalation path, and next milestone. Explain how
requests become changes and what pauses the timeline. Send a written recap; memory is not a shared
system.

## Transfer access safely

Request only access necessary for the current phase. Use approved credential-sharing methods,
record owners, verify permissions, and define when temporary access is revoked. Never ask a client
to paste passwords into chat or email. Confirm environment, repository, domain, hosting, and
third-party ownership before work depends on them.

## Start with a mutual commitment

Close onboarding with one deliverable from each side and a near-term checkpoint. The first milestone
should expose dependency problems early and establish the communication rhythm before expensive
work accumulates.

## What good looks like

Both sides can state the outcome, boundaries, responsibilities, approval path, and next milestone in
the same terms. Work begins only after commercial and access prerequisites are satisfied. Scope
changes have a known route, client-caused delays are visible, and no critical credential or decision
depends on an informal message.

## Hard rules

- NEVER start delivery before the agreement and required payment are complete.
- NEVER leave content, access, feedback, or approval as an ownerless dependency.
- NEVER accept credentials through insecure ad hoc channels.
- ALWAYS name one accountable client approver.
- ALWAYS document scope changes with their price and timeline effect.
