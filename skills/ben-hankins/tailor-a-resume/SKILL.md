**When to use this skill:** a real candidate is applying to a defined role and their strongest
evidence must become obvious without distortion.

**What it produces:** a role thesis, requirement-to-evidence map, prioritized achievement bullets,
gap strategy, keyword coverage, and a concise tailored resume.

## Read the role as a scorecard

Separate outcomes, recurring responsibilities, required proof, domain constraints, and screening
language. Rank them by importance and infer the problem behind the opening. Ignore generic employer
adjectives unless the candidate has evidence that changes the case.

## Build the evidence map first

Map each priority to a verified example with action, scope, constraint, result, and measurement.
Recover evidence from projects, incidents, portfolios, promotions, and adjacent work—not only job
titles. Mark strong, transferable, and missing proof. Never begin by rewriting sentences from the
old resume.

## Lead with the hiring thesis

Choose the two or three capabilities that best explain why this candidate fits now. Order summary,
skills, roles, and bullets to reinforce that case. Translate internal terminology into language the
reader recognizes while preserving factual accuracy.

## Write achievements with consequence

Use action, context, and result; quantify scale, speed, quality, revenue, cost, risk, or adoption
where records support it. Do not manufacture precision. Combine related activity into one outcome
and cut duties that neither differentiate the candidate nor prove a requirement.

## Handle gaps honestly

Address transferable experience, relevant learning, or adjacent results directly. Do not disguise
missing credentials or inflate ownership. When a requirement is truly absent, improve the
application strategy or close the gap rather than keyword-stuffing.

## What good looks like

A reviewer can identify the role thesis and strongest evidence in seconds. Each major claim is
defensible in an interview, keywords appear naturally around real proof, and the resume shows
consequence rather than a task inventory. Vertical conventions shape emphasis without creating a
different underlying standard of truth.

## Hard rules

- NEVER invent metrics, credentials, tools, titles, or ownership.
- NEVER copy job-description language without evidence.
- NEVER preserve a bullet merely because it appeared on the old resume.
- ALWAYS map priority requirements to verified proof before drafting.
- ALWAYS optimize for human comprehension as well as screening systems.
