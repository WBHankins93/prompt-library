**When to use this skill:** a purchase is costly or hard to reverse and the asking price hides
ongoing cost, condition, concentration, or exit risk.

**What it produces:** decision criteria, total-cost and downside models, diligence plan, uncertainty
map, negotiation boundaries, walk-away conditions, and recommendation.

## Define value before reviewing the asset

State the job the purchase must do, constraints, holding period, alternatives, and acceptable
downside. Separate needs from preferences and affordability from value. Set the maximum exposure
before attachment to a specific asset.

## Reconstruct total ownership

Include acquisition, financing, taxes, insurance, maintenance, repair, operating labor, transition,
opportunity cost, and exit friction. Model base and downside cash. For income assets, rebuild
earnings from source records and normalize owner labor, deferred maintenance, and one-time items.

## Diligence the failure modes

Inspect condition, title or ownership, contracts, dependencies, regulation, concentration,
insurability, and information controlled by the seller. Use qualified specialists where the
consequence exceeds general judgment. Treat refusal, inconsistency, and urgency as evidence.

## Price uncertainty explicitly

Distinguish verified fact, estimate, and unknown. Seek evidence for load-bearing assumptions; reduce
price, change terms, stage the commitment, or walk away when uncertainty remains. A favorable
average case does not compensate for ruinous tail risk.

## Negotiate from boundaries

Tie requests to evidence, cost, and risk rather than enthusiasm. Define target, maximum, required
conditions, and walk-away triggers in advance. Preserve optionality until diligence and financing
conditions are satisfied.

## What good looks like

The buyer understands total ownership, downside liquidity, and exit—not only monthly payment or
headline multiple. Material unknowns have a price or condition, diligence matches consequence, and
walking away remains emotionally and financially available.

## Hard rules

- NEVER confuse lender approval with affordability.
- NEVER rely on seller summaries when source records are available.
- NEVER average away a catastrophic downside.
- ALWAYS set walk-away conditions before negotiation.
- ALWAYS price or condition material uncertainty.
