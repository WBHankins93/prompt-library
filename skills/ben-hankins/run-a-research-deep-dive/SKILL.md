**When to use this skill:** the answer will influence a consequential decision and evidence quality,
contradiction, or uncertainty matters as much as the conclusion.

**What it produces:** a scoped question, evidence map, confidence-graded synthesis, contradictions,
unknowns, implications, and auditable source trail.

## Turn the topic into answerable claims

Define the decision, audience, time horizon, geography, and exclusions. Break the question into
claims whose truth would change the recommendation. State current hypotheses without treating them
as findings.

## Design the source strategy

Prioritize primary evidence, official data, methods, and direct records; use strong secondary
sources for context and discovery. Seek independent sources with different incentives. Match source
freshness to how quickly the fact changes and record access dates where necessary.

## Extract evidence, not summaries

For each claim, capture the evidence, method, population, limits, and source. Distinguish observed
fact, author interpretation, and your inference. Follow citations to their origin and avoid counting
several articles that repeat one study as independent confirmation.

## Reconcile contradiction

When sources disagree, compare definitions, samples, dates, incentives, and methods before choosing
a side. Preserve unresolved conflict and explain what evidence would settle it. Absence of a result
may reflect missing measurement rather than no effect.

## Synthesize for the decision

Grade confidence claim by claim, identify robust conclusions and fragile dependencies, then connect
them to options and consequences. State unknowns and the value of additional research. Stop when
new sources no longer change material claims.

## What good looks like

A skeptical reader can trace each important conclusion to evidence, understand why contradictory
sources differ, and see where inference begins. Confidence varies appropriately across claims, and
the synthesis changes a decision rather than merely displaying information.

## Hard rules

- NEVER count repeated reporting of one source as independent evidence.
- NEVER hide contradictory evidence or material uncertainty.
- NEVER cite a source you did not inspect.
- ALWAYS distinguish fact, source interpretation, and your inference.
- ALWAYS stop when further research has low decision value.
