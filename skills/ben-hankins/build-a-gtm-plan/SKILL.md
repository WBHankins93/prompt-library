**When to use this skill:** a valuable offer exists but "more marketing" is standing in for a
testable theory of who buys, why now, and how the company reaches them repeatedly.

**What it produces:** a market and buyer thesis, positioning, channel and sales sequence, funnel
model, and a short experiment backlog with kill criteria.

## Choose a market narrow enough to learn

Define a specific buyer, painful job, triggering event, current alternative, and cost of doing
nothing. Prefer a segment where the problem clusters and buyers recognize one another. A broad TAM
does not compensate for weak urgency. State who is deliberately not the first customer.

## Make the promise testable

Position around the outcome, the alternative displaced, and the evidence that makes the claim
credible. Avoid category language that requires the buyer to translate features into value. Name the
reason to act now and the constraint that makes the offer a poor fit for some buyers.

## Match motion to buying reality

Choose self-serve, founder-led, sales-assisted, partner, or enterprise motion based on price, risk,
complexity, and number of stakeholders. Design the smallest channel sequence that reaches the buyer
where intent already exists. Do not run five channels before one message earns response.

## Model the whole path

Write the assumptions from reachable audience through activation, conversion, retention, and
expansion. Use ranges and identify the assumption that most changes the economics. Leading
indicators must connect to revenue or retained use; impressions and activity are not progress by
themselves.

## Run falsifiable experiments

Test the riskiest assumption with the cheapest credible evidence: interviews with commitment,
targeted outreach, paid demand, landing-page conversion, or a manually delivered offer. Define the
threshold and next action before results arrive. Scale only after a message-channel-segment
combination repeats.

## What good looks like

The plan can explain who buys, what event creates urgency, why the offer wins against the current
alternative, and how one channel reaches that buyer economically. Metrics expose the weakest
assumption, and the next experiment can disprove the thesis within weeks rather than protecting it
for quarters.

## Hard rules

- NEVER use market size as evidence of reachable demand.
- NEVER scale channels before one segment-message combination repeats.
- NEVER treat attention metrics as revenue evidence without a conversion path.
- ALWAYS state the first customer and the deliberate non-customer.
- ALWAYS define experiment thresholds before observing results.
