**When to use this skill:** discovery is sufficient to define the outcome and you need to price the
work without hiding uncertainty or training the client to negotiate scope through discounts.

**What it produces:** a selected pricing model, protected price floor, outcome-led proposal,
payment schedule, bounded options, and explicit next steps.

## Match the model to uncertainty

Use fixed price when deliverables, dependencies, and acceptance are stable. Use hourly or a bounded
discovery phase when the work cannot yet be estimated honestly. Use a retainer for recurring
capacity with defined deliverables or hours. Use value-based pricing only when the outcome is
measurable, attribution is credible, and both sides understand the assumptions. Pricing format
cannot repair vague scope.

## Calculate the floor before the offer

Include delivery time, project management, revision risk, non-billable overhead, third-party cost,
contingency, taxes, and the opportunity cost of constrained capacity. Add risk for uncertainty you
are accepting, not arbitrary padding. Record the floor privately so negotiation starts from an
economic boundary rather than anxiety.

## Write from the client's problem

Open with the current state and desired outcome in the client's language. Describe the proposed
approach, included scope, exclusions, assumptions, dependencies, success criteria, and timeline.
Explain investment after value and scope are legible. Avoid implementation detail that does not
change cost, risk, ownership, or the result.

## Offer meaningful choices

Lead with one recommendation. If budget is genuinely uncertain, offer at most one smaller option
with less scope, speed, or support. Make the trade visible. Several near-identical packages shift
the design work to the buyer and obscure what you believe will succeed.

## Protect cash flow and change

Tie payments to time or milestones so unpaid exposure stays bounded. Require an initial payment,
define late and pause conditions, and price out-of-scope work through written change orders. Do not
make final payment contingent on indefinite subjective satisfaction.

## Make acceptance operational

State proposal validity, the exact action to accept, agreement and payment prerequisites, and what
happens within the first business day after acceptance. A proposal without a start path is marketing
material, not a commercial instrument.

## What good looks like

The buyer can connect price to a specific outcome and understand what changes under the smaller
option. The provider knows the floor and exposure, the payment schedule funds the work, and scope
changes cannot silently erase margin. Acceptance leads directly into an executable onboarding step.

## Hard rules

- NEVER use fixed price to disguise scope that is not understood.
- NEVER lower price without changing scope, risk, timing, or terms.
- NEVER present more than two options.
- ALWAYS calculate a private price floor before negotiating.
- ALWAYS make acceptance, payment, and the first next step explicit.
