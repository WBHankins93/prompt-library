**When to use this skill:** a stakeholder raises price, timing, risk, competition, or uncertainty
and answering the words alone may miss what actually blocks the decision.

**What it produces:** an objection diagnosis, a proportionate evidence-based response, and a
specific next commitment or disqualification decision.

## Receive the objection as information

Do not interrupt, rebut, or immediately defend. Restate the concern neutrally and ask what changed
or what the stakeholder would need to believe. Objections often bundle several issues, and the first
phrase is frequently the socially easy one. "Too expensive" may mean unproven value, no budget
authority, or a safer competing priority.

## Classify the actual obstacle

Distinguish among:

- **Value:** the outcome does not justify cost or disruption.
- **Evidence:** the claim is not yet credible in this environment.
- **Risk:** security, reliability, adoption, or career exposure feels too high.
- **Process:** budget, timing, procurement, or approval mechanics block progress.
- **Access:** the current contact cannot win the internal decision.
- **Fit:** a real requirement cannot be met.

Each class requires a different move. More product detail does not repair a process or access gap.

## Test the diagnosis

Ask a conditional question: if this concern were resolved, would anything else prevent the next
step? Map who owns the concern and who can accept the answer. Avoid treating a messenger as the
final authority. When the objection is vague, request a recent example, threshold, or decision rule.

## Respond with evidence and honest trades

Use observed results, agreed success criteria, comparable experience, or a bounded validation plan.
For real limitations, state the boundary and its consequence, then offer a mitigation only if it is
operationally credible. For price, reconnect to quantified value or reduce scope; do not discount
the same promise merely to end discomfort.

## Convert resolution into a commitment

Agree on the next decision, owner, and date: introduce the approver, validate a risk, revise scope,
or close the opportunity. If new objections appear indefinitely or the requirement is truly
unmet, disqualify rather than manufacturing activity that resembles progress.

## What good looks like

The stakeholder feels accurately understood, the real obstacle is named, and the response uses
evidence at the level of the concern. Resolution creates a concrete change in deal state. When fit
is absent, the team exits honestly without pressure, false urgency, or a concession that creates a
bad customer.

## Hard rules

- NEVER treat an objection as an argument to win.
- NEVER use false urgency, manipulation, or a promise the product cannot keep.
- NEVER discount before diagnosing the value or scope gap.
- ALWAYS verify that resolving the stated concern would change the decision.
- ALWAYS disqualify when a load-bearing requirement cannot be met.
