**When to use this skill:** a venture opportunity is attractive enough to deserve disciplined
evaluation before enthusiasm, sunk cost, or narrative becomes commitment.

**What it produces:** a viability thesis, economics and distribution assessment, ranked risks and
evidence gaps, staged validation plan, and invest, test, or pass recommendation.

## Prove the painful problem

Define the specific user, recurring job, current workaround, frequency, urgency, and consequence.
Separate compliments and stated interest from costly behavior: time spent, money paid, data shared,
or process changed. Name why the problem is becoming more important now.

## Evaluate access and distribution

Assess how the venture reaches buyers, earns trust, navigates the buying process, and repeats
acquisition. Include founder access, channel concentration, sales cycle, and switching friction.
Product quality cannot rescue an acquisition path that is unaffordable or structurally inaccessible.

## Rebuild the economics

Model price, gross margin, acquisition cost, retention, expansion, delivery burden, working capital,
and cash timing. For acquisitions, normalize owner compensation and one-time add-backs; for new
ventures, distinguish expected scale economics from current evidence. Identify what must be true for
the return to justify risk.

## Test execution fit and defensibility

Compare the capability, credibility, time, and capital required with what the team possesses.
Evaluate defensibility as durable access, workflow embedding, data advantage, network, brand, cost,
or switching—not merely being first. Name dependencies and regulatory or platform exposure.

## Stage the commitment

Rank unknowns by ability to kill the case. Design the cheapest evidence for each: paid pilot,
pre-commitment, cohort behavior, channel test, technical spike, or diligence request. Commit capital
in stages with thresholds and a stop condition.

## What good looks like

The recommendation rests on evidence of painful demand, a reachable customer, coherent economics,
and a team capable of the next stage. Unknowns are not hidden in a single confidence score. The
next commitment buys specific information, and passing remains an acceptable outcome.

## Hard rules

- NEVER confuse a large market with reachable demand.
- NEVER treat founder passion as evidence of customer urgency.
- NEVER accept adjusted economics without reconstructing cash reality.
- ALWAYS test distribution before assuming product quality will create it.
- ALWAYS define what evidence makes the opportunity a pass.
